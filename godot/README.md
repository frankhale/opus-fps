# Godot project

A walkable PS1-style test level built from the asset pack. Open this folder in Godot 4.7 and press F5, or run:

```
/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot --path godot
```

**Controls:** WASD or arrow keys to move, mouse to look, Space to jump (hold for higher), Shift to sprint, and C or Ctrl to crouch. Crouching while sprinting starts a slide. Click to fire, and use 1–5 or the mouse wheel to switch weapons. Esc releases the mouse and clicking recaptures it. The game starts fullscreen; F11, Cmd+Ctrl+F or Alt+Enter toggles windowed mode (macOS uses F11 for Show Desktop by default, so on a Mac use Cmd+Ctrl+F or turn that shortcut off). M switches between the corner minimap and a large map. Cmd+M hides or shows the map. F1 toggles the PS1 filter, F2 shows the controls help (hidden at start), Cmd+1 switches between the game and the model gallery, F12 saves a screenshot (with the HUD) to `~/Pictures/Opus FPS/`, ~ opens the dev console, and Cmd+Q quits. On a Mac keyboard, press Fn+F12 unless the F-keys are set to act as standard function keys.

**Barrels** (plain and explosive) are loose physics objects. Shots knock them about where they hit (harder for stronger weapons, so a shotgun blast bowls one over), and rocket splash and gas-can blasts throw them into the air. The red **explosive barrels** also blow up like gas cans, with a bigger blast (5.5 m). They take the same hits to set off, and one thrown by a blast can go up in mid-air.

**Gems** are tucked around every landmark (21 in all, a few in the creek and on the pond bridges). Walk into one to pick it up for good. Killed enemies drop more: one from each floating eye, three from each golem, which blink and vanish if not picked up within 30 seconds. The HUD shows a spinning gem with your count next to it ("x 5"), just above the health bar.

**Gas cans** blow up when shot: three pistol rounds or one hit from any other weapon (each weapon's `gas_can_shots`). The blast hurts you and enemies within 4 m, sets off other cans and explosive barrels nearby, and leaves the fuel burning for 10 seconds, dying down over the last 4. Rocket splash sets them off too.

**Dev console:** ~ (the key left of 1) slides a see-through console up over the bottom quarter of the screen and pauses the game; ~ or Esc closes it. Type a command and press Enter; Up/Down recall earlier ones. `help` lists commands, `clear` empties the log, and `god` toggles god mode: no damage, plus every weapon and unlimited ammo when it turns on (`god 1` / `god 0` set it).

## Layout

| Path | What it is |
|------|------------|
| `scenes/main.tscn` | Entry scene. Renders the world in a low-res `SubViewport` (about 480 lines), upscaled with nearest filtering and run through the dither shader. |
| `scenes/world.tscn` | Sky, fog, sun, the level and the player. The mood is a clear midday: a blue sky fading to a pale horizon with fair-weather clouds drifting across it (`materials/sky.gdshader`), a warm sun about 42° up that casts shadows (four cascades out to 140 m), cool blue fill light taken partly from the sky so shade reads blue rather than grey, light blue-grey distance fog that glows faintly toward the sun, and a slight brightness and saturation lift. Screen-space AO is deliberately off: at the low render resolution it crushes whole shadowed areas to black. A thin volumetric haze softens the distance, and the `GroundMist` fog volume adds a light mist that pools near the ground and thins with height. |
| `scenes/level.tscn` | The level itself: a 184 × 184 m valley of rolling hills ringed by cliffs and mountains. Pines grow in groves with open meadows between them, so you can see across the land. A creek (`terrain_creek` tiles) crosses the south of the valley from cliff to cliff along z = 64, just south of the outpost and the homestead. It flows west to east, the main road crosses it on a wooden bridge, and it cuts a small gorge through the rising rim at each end. Coils of barbed wire line the front and west side of the outpost, narrow the west road into a roadblock and lie about the ruins. It's generated by `tools/build_level.gd` but is an ordinary scene you can edit. The builder scales scenery up from the pack's real-world sizes (`WORLD_SCALE`): trees about 2–2.4×, cliffs 2×, ruins 1.6×. Things the player interacts with stay near human scale. |
| `scenes/gallery.tscn`, `scripts/gallery.gd` | Model gallery: the game with its level swapped for a grassy field (a Terrain of `terrain_grass` tiles with the hills off) showing every model in `assets/`, one row per category (weapons, pickups, ammo, viewmodel, enemies, scenery, terrain, front to back). Each row has its category name above it and each model its file name. Small categories are enlarged (the heading says by how much) and weapons are turned side-on. The campfire burns with the game's fire effect (`scripts/campfire.gd`), and the golem floats on its electric arcs. Models, names and headings are set onto the grass's gentle bumps. The air is clear: the world's fog, haze and ground mist are off there. A wooden fence runs round the edge of the field to show where it ends, backed by invisible walls so you can't jump it and fall off. It reads the asset folders when it loads, so new models appear on their own. Cmd+1 switches to it from the game and back. You can also open it and press F6, or run `Godot --path godot res://scenes/gallery.tscn`. `main.gd`'s `level_scene` is what swaps the level in. |
| `scenes/player.tscn`, `scripts/player.gd` | FPS movement (CharacterBody3D): quick acceleration and braking, air control that keeps momentum, sprint, crouch, sprint-slide, a jump with coyote time, input buffering and variable height, and stepping up ledges up to 35 cm. It emits `jumped`, `landed`, `slide_started` and `slide_ended`. |
| `scripts/camera_effects.gd` | Camera feel, purely visual: head bob with sway and foot plants, leaning into strafes and slides, FOV kick when sprinting and sliding, springy landing dip, shake on hard landings and idle breathing. |
| `scripts/player_audio.gd` | Footsteps synced to the bob, plus jump, landing and slide sounds. |
| `scripts/weapons/weapon_manager.gd` | Weapons inventory, ammo (bullets, shells, rockets, cells, each with a carry limit), firing and the held weapon. The held weapon is drawn by its own camera into an overlay so it never clips into walls, and it sways, bobs, kicks and switches with animation. First-person arms (`assets/viewmodel/`) hold each weapon. Every `WeaponData` sets where the right hand grips and whether the left hand cradles a barrel or handguard (`support`) or holds a front grip (`grip`). The forearms run from each wrist to a shoulder point just below the view, so the arms follow sway, recoil and switching. |
| `weapons/*.tres` | One `WeaponData` per weapon (pistol, shotgun, rifle, rocket launcher, plasma rifle): model and hold pose, fire rate, pellets, spread, recoil, ammo and projectile look. Each projectile's `projectile_size` (radius) drives its trail, muzzle flash and impact explosion, so they all scale together: shotgun pellet < pistol < rifle < plasma < rocket. |
| `scripts/weapons/projectile.gd`, `explosion.gd`, `fx.gd` | Glowing projectiles with particle trails (and smoke for rockets) that raycast each step. Rockets have a proximity fuse (`proximity_radius`) that sets them off when they pass within 1.5 m of an enemy, so they still catch fast, strafing eyes, plus explosions (fireball, smoke, sparks, light flash, sound, camera shake) scaled by the weapon. |
| `scripts/physics_prop.gd` | Makes the barrels physics objects. On load, each swaps its imported static collision for a `RigidBody3D` with a cylinder fitted to the model, which carries the mesh. It stands frozen until first hit, so barrels on slopes don't roll away by themselves and idle ones cost nothing. Projectiles push any rigid body they hit at the point of impact (`KNOCK_PER_DAMAGE` in `projectile.gd`). `blast()` throws props up and away from rocket splash and gas-can explosions, capped at `MAX_THROW_SPEED` so several blasts at once don't send one out of sight. `tools/build_level.gd` puts it on every `barrel`. |
| `scripts/explosive_barrel.gd` | `PhysicsProp` for `barrel_explosive`: knocked about like the plain barrels, and blows up like a gas can (`gas_can_shots` hits, or any blast nearby), with a 1.4× fireball, fire and smoke. The blast itself is `GasCan.fuel_blast()` and `GasCan.blast_at()`. Cans and barrels share the `explosives` group, so either sets the other off. |
| `scripts/weapons/pickup.gd` | Weapon, ammo and health pickups that respawn (weapons after 30 s, ammo after 15 s, health after 25 s), and gems, which glow green and are taken for good with a chime and a sparkle. They're placed by `tools/build_level.gd`. Enemy loot uses the same pickup as a one-time drop that blinks out after 30 s. |
| `scripts/enemies/enemy.gd` | `Enemy`, the base class of both enemies: noticing the player (sight range and line of sight) and keeping them in mind for a few seconds after losing sight, the hit flash and knockback, death, loot (ammo for weapons you own, or health, plus gems: `gem_drops`, 1 per eye and 3 per golem; drops blink and vanish if not taken within 30 s, `loot_lifetime`) and respawning at home out of the player's sight. |
| `scripts/enemies/floating_eye.gd` | The floating eye enemy. It hovers near home until it sees the player, then screeches and chases, holding about 11 m away and strafing. It glows and whines before spitting a slow, dodgeable bolt (`weapons/enemy_eye_bolt.tres`). Its rigged tentacles are animated in code: a wave runs down each one, they trail behind as it moves, flare out while it charges and twitch when hit. It has 80 health, flashes and recoils when hit, bursts on death, drops ammo for weapons you own or health plus a gem, and respawns at home after 45 s. Nine are placed around the map. |
| `scripts/enemies/golem.gd` | The rock golem: a slow, heavy brute with no legs, floating 0.85 m up on an electric charge that crackles from its hips to the ground, with a low hum. Once it sees you it drifts after you. Out of reach it throws a ball of lightning (`weapons/enemy_golem_bolt.tres`, 16 damage). Within 4 m it gathers its charge, sinking toward the ground, and discharges a shock ring that does up to 30 damage within 5 m, throws you back, scatters barrels and sets off gas cans. Both attacks are telegraphed: arcs crawl over its body, its crack and eyes glow blue and the buzz rises. It has 320 health, barely moves when shot, bursts into rubble on death, drops 2–3 pickups and 3 gems, and respawns after 60 s. Three guard the north: the ruins, the graveyard and the north pond. |
| `scripts/enemies/electric_arcs.gd` | `ElectricArcs`: jagged lightning bolts from points on a body, fanning out down to the ground, redrawn about 16 times a second so they jump and flicker. Each is drawn as a white-hot core inside a blue glow and a wide soft halo, with a glow where they land, sparks, wisps of pale smoke and a flickering light. `intensity` above 1 thickens them and sets them crawling over the body. The golem floats on it, and its shock ring uses it too. |
| `scripts/reticle.gd` | Aiming reticle. Its shape follows the weapon (`reticle` in `WeaponData`: cross, circle or dot), and its gap is the weapon's true current spread. Spread grows with each shot and while moving or airborne, then recovers when you stop firing. It turns red over an enemy and shows hit markers: a white X on a hit, a red X on a kill, with ticks. |
| `scripts/hud.gd`, `scripts/health_bar.gd`, `scripts/ammo_display.gd`, `scripts/gem_counter.gd` | The gem count above the health bar: the gem pickup model spinning in a small low-res 3D view, then "x N". The count pops and the gem spins fast when you pick one up. A health bar that fades from green through yellow to red as health drops (each hit leaves a pale chunk that drains away), the current weapon, pickup messages, a red flash when hurt and a death screen. Ammo is shown as rows of 3D rounds (`assets/ammo/`), one per round, in a small low-resolution view. Spent rounds hop away, new ones grow in, the rounds turn red when low, and any count beyond what fits shows as "+N". The player has 100 health and respawns at the start point 3 s after dying. |
| `assets/sfx/` | Lo-fi sound effects synthesized by `../tools/generate_sfx.py`, including the ambient wind loop played by `world.tscn`. |
| `scripts/terrain.gd` | The ground (`Terrain/Ground` in the level). It lays the pack's 8 m terrain tiles out on a 23 × 23 grid and bends them over gently rolling hills: a smooth, seeded noise height field added to every vertex. The hills grow taller away from the middle, and the valley floor sweeps up toward the cliffs. Hills fade out over the flat zones (villages, camps, ponds, the spawn and the portals) and are softened along the roads, so the road tiles simply follow the land. The meshes and their collision are built when the level loads (in about a quarter of a second, and also in the editor), so the scene stores only the tile layout and settings. `height(x, z)` gives the hill height anywhere; the level builder uses it to drop props onto the slopes. The ground is drawn at 80% brightness (`ground_brightness`), so the sunlit grass doesn't outshine the props. With `uniform_ground` on (the default), each surface is one flat shade: all grass one green, all road dirt one brown and all sand one tan. That replaces the tiles' checkerboard of light and dark faces. Creek water (`PS1_Water`) has no collision, so you wade along the creek bed, and keeps its flow UVs. `creek_z` holds the ground flat along a creek, the rising rim included, so its water lies level. |
| `scripts/campfire.gd` | Burning campfire effect, added on top of each campfire model by `tools/build_level.gd` (at the campsite and the outpost). It has alpha-blended flame tongues with a white-hot additive core, so they read against the bright daytime scene. Embers drift up on turbulence and wink out, and smoke billows and thins as it rises, drifting with `wind`. A warm firelight flickers. It's built from the shared particle pieces in `scripts/weapons/fx.gd` and also burns in the editor. |
| `scripts/portal.gd`, `materials/portal.gdshader` | Linked portals: a stone ring gate (`assets/scenery/portal.glb`) filled with a shimmering, see-through membrane of blue light. The shader copies the scene behind the membrane, bends it slightly and lays swirling, rippling blue bands and sparks over it. The center is mostly clear and the rim glows solid. Walking through one portal's membrane sends you out of its `exit_path` portal and keeps your offset, facing and momentum: go in the front and you come out the exit's front, go in the back and you come out its back. The view stretches briefly, a whoosh plays, and both portals flare. The frame is solid (its collision is built at runtime from the stone surfaces only). Each portal hums and gives off a pulsing blue light, and the gold runes on its ring (two studs and the keystone on each face) slowly pulse their glow, flaring when someone passes through. `tools/build_level.gd` places a linked pair: `PortalWest`, 50 m west along the crossroads road, and `PortalNorth`, past the ruins on the main road. |
| `materials/sky.gdshader` | The sky in `world.tscn`. It draws the same gradient, sun disc and below-horizon ground as Godot's `ProceduralSkyMaterial` (with the same parameters), plus a layer of clouds made of fractal noise that drift with `wind`. Their density is posterized into a few flat steps for the PS1 look. The side facing the sun is lit and the far side shaded grey. Clouds thin out toward the horizon and pass in front of the sun. Coverage, softness, cloud size, colors and wind are shader parameters. The sky is set to update in real time, since the clouds move. |
| `materials/water.gdshader` | Flowing creek water (`materials/ps1_water.tres`, on `terrain_creek`'s `PS1_Water` sheet). Streaky ripples scroll downstream in two layers, posterized into flat color steps with pale crests. The bed shows through and wobbles with the ripples, and the water darkens with depth. White foam hugs the rocks and banks, read from the depth buffer. Flow speed, colors, murkiness and foam width are shader parameters. |
| `scripts/minimap.gd` | North-up minimap: a top-down render of the level taken at startup. The corner view scrolls with the player, M shows the whole map, an arrow shows position and facing, and red dots mark living enemies. |
| `materials/ps1.gdshader` | Surface shader for the models: vertex snapping to the render's pixel grid, affine texture mapping, nearest-neighbour palette sampling and per-vertex lighting. It corrects each pixel's depth toward the face's true plane, so snapping can't make nearly coplanar surfaces (windows, trim) z-fight. The correction is capped (`depth_fix_slack`, 1% of the distance plus 3 cm). On faces seen edge-on and along silhouettes, the true plane can lie far from the snapped triangle, and an uncapped correction there opened see-through cracks between faces. That requires the depth pre-pass to be off (`rendering/driver/depth_prepass/enable`). |
| `materials/ps1_post.gdshader` | Screen shader: 15-bit color with 4×4 ordered dithering. |
| `assets/` | Copies of the GLBs from the repo's `assets/` folder. |

Every model imports with its `PS1_Palette` and `PS1_Glow` materials replaced by `materials/ps1_palette.tres` and `materials/ps1_glow.tres`. Portal membranes (`PS1_Portal`) use `materials/ps1_portal.tres`. Terrain and solid scenery also get a static trimesh collision body. These settings live in each model's `.import` file.

## Updating assets

After regenerating the asset pack (see the root `README.md`), run:

```
python3 tools/sync_godot_assets.py                                              # copy models, write import settings
/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot --headless --path godot --import   # reimport
/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot --headless --path godot --script res://tools/build_level.gd   # optional: rebuild the level
```

Rebuilding the level overwrites `scenes/level.tscn`, including any hand edits.
