# Godot project

A walkable PS1-style test level built from the asset pack. Open this folder in Godot 4.7 and press F5, or run:

```
/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot --path godot
```

**Controls:** WASD or arrow keys to move, mouse to look, Space to jump (hold for higher, about 1.7 m; jump into a ledge while pushing toward it to vault up onto it), Shift to sprint, and C or Ctrl to crouch. Crouching while sprinting starts a slide. Click to fire, and use 1–5 or the mouse wheel to switch weapons. Esc releases the mouse and clicking recaptures it. M switches between the corner minimap and a large map, ~ opens the dev console, and Cmd+S (Ctrl+S off macOS) saves a screenshot, HUD included, to `~/Documents`. There are no other shortcut keys: fullscreen (the game starts fullscreen), the PS1 filter, hiding the map, switching level, other screenshots and quitting are dev console commands (below).

**Barrels** (plain and explosive) are loose physics objects. Shots knock them about where they hit (harder for stronger weapons, so a shotgun blast bowls one over), and rocket splash and gas-can blasts throw them into the air. The red **explosive barrels** also blow up like gas cans, with a bigger blast (5.5 m). They take the same hits to set off, and one thrown by a blast can go up in mid-air.

**Weapons** other than the pistol (which waits just ahead of the spawn and comes back there 30 s after it's taken) turn up at random spots around the valley: new ones every game, and a new one each time a weapon respawns. The spots are on open, fairly level ground, never in water, under a roof or tree, or up against anything, and spread out from each other and from you. Pick up a second pistol to hold one in each hand: they fire in turn, right then left.

**Invulnerability** (`powerup_invulnerability`, the caged cyan orb) also sits at a random spot. Picking it up makes you immune to all damage for 15 seconds (blasts still push you about). A shimmering cyan veil wraps around you while it lasts: rippling lines that drift across the view as you turn, keeping the middle of the screen clear for aiming. It hums, flares toward any hit it turns away, flickers over the last 3 seconds and fades out with a falling chime. The HUD shows "INVULNERABLE" and the seconds left at the top. It comes back 60 s after it's taken, somewhere new.

**Gems** are tucked around every landmark (22 in all, a few in the creek, on the pond bridges and one on the checkpoint bridge). Walk into one to pick it up for good. Killed enemies drop more: one from each floating eye or spider, three from each golem, which blink and vanish if not picked up within 30 seconds. The HUD shows a spinning gem with your count next to it ("x 5"), just above the health bar.

**Watchtowers** stand at the campsite, the outpost and a checkpoint on the west road, where two face each other across the road with a plank bridge between their decks, 7 m up. Every tower's ladder can be climbed: walk into it and push forward to climb up, pull back to climb down, and push on at the top to step over onto the deck. Mid-climb you can't back off it, so you don't fall off by accident. Strafing shuffles you slowly sideways (you drift back to the middle when you stop), and shuffling more than 25 cm off center steps you off the side to fall. You can't look down more than 30° while climbing. Walking off the deck at the ladder's railing gap takes you onto the ladder and down. Jump to let go. On a ladder your weapon is put away (no firing) and you climb hand over hand: each gloved hand grips a real rung and holds it as you climb past, then reaches up for the next, and near the top they reach onto the edge of the deck. The hands slide along the rungs as you shuffle sideways, so your arms never stretch out. Stepping off brings the weapon back up.

**Gas cans** blow up when shot: three pistol rounds or one hit from any other weapon (each weapon's `gas_can_shots`). The blast hurts you and enemies within 4 m, sets off other cans and explosive barrels nearby, and leaves the fuel burning for 10 seconds, dying down over the last 4. Rocket splash sets them off too.

**Dev console:** ~ (the key left of 1) slides a see-through console up over the bottom quarter of the screen and pauses the game; ~ or Esc closes it. Type a command and press Enter; Up/Down recall earlier ones. Toggles flip with no argument, and `1` / `0` (or `on` / `off`) set them.

| Command | What it does |
|---------|--------------|
| `help` | Lists the commands. |
| `clear` | Empties the log. |
| `god` | God mode: no damage, plus every weapon and unlimited ammo when it turns on. |
| `aggro` | Enemy aggro. Off, enemies never notice you, even when you shoot them: any hunting you give up (the shadow cat calls off a swing already under way), their aggro rings disappear and they go back to roaming. Shots already in the air can still hit you. |
| `sfx` | Sound effects, which is all the game's audio, the wind and surf included. They start off (the Master bus is muted in `default_bus_layout.tres`). |
| `ps1` | The PS1 look: the low-res render, dithering and 15-bit color. |
| `fullscreen` | Fullscreen or windowed. |
| `map` | Hides or shows the map (M still switches between the corner and large map). |
| `cat` | Sends the shadow cat off at once at a given gait, to watch it: `cat walk`, `cat trot` or `cat run`. It only works on the island, where the cat lives, and not while it's hunting you. |
| `scene` | Switches level: `scene hub` (where the game starts), `scene world` (the valley), `scene gallery` or `scene island`. On its own it names the current one. The game reloads with the new level. |
| `screenshot` | Closes the console and saves a screenshot (with the HUD) to `~/Pictures/Opus FPS/`. |
| `quit` | Quits the game. |

`aggro`, `sfx`, `ps1` and `map` hold when you switch level.

## Prefabs

`prefabs/` is a library of every object in the game, each a ready-to-use scene: drag one from the FileSystem panel into any level and it works as it does everywhere else. The levels are built from them.

| Folder | What's in it |
|--------|--------------|
| `prefabs/scenery/` | Every scenery model at its game size (the levels show the pack's real-size models bigger: trees most, things you handle near human size). Those with a behaviour have it: `barrel` (knocked about by shots), `barrel_explosive` and `gas_can` (blow up), `campfire` (burning), `watchtower` (with its climbable `Ladder`), `lamp_post` (with its lantern's `Light`). `portal` is a portal: set its exit, or a `level` and `title`, in the Inspector. |
| `prefabs/terrain/` | The terrain tiles, cliffs and mountains. |
| `prefabs/pickups/` | `weapon_<name>`, `ammo_<type>`, `health_medkit`, `health_vial`, `gem` and `powerup_invulnerability`, each with its amount, model and respawn time. |
| `prefabs/enemies/` | `floating_eye`, `golem`, `spider` and `shadow_cat`. |
| `prefabs/creatures/` | The harmless island creatures: `lynx_ranger`, `dachshund`, `arcane_eye`, `jellyfish` and `lovebird`. |

The model prefabs are inherited scenes of their `.glb`, so a regenerated model shows up in every prefab and level that uses it. Enemies and creatures build their model when the game runs, so each carries an `EditorPreview` (`scripts/editor_preview.gd`) that shows it in the editor only. Change a prefab and every level using it follows; change one placed copy (its settings in the Inspector, or Editable Children for its parts) and only that copy differs.

`tools/build_prefabs.gd` makes the library: it adds prefabs for new models and leaves existing ones alone (`-- --force` rebuilds them all from scratch). `tools/use_prefabs.gd` converts a scene's objects (placed straight from models, or set up by hand from a script) into prefab instances, keeping their names, transforms and settings: `Godot --headless --path godot --script res://tools/use_prefabs.gd -- res://scenes/<scene>.tscn`.

## Layout

| Path | What it is |
|------|------------|
| `scenes/main.tscn` | Entry scene: the game around a level. The world, HUD and dev console are the same in every level. On start it loads the chosen level (`Main.level`, one of `main.gd`'s `LEVELS`: the hub, where the game starts, or the valley, the gallery or the island) into the world, ahead of the player. `Main.change_level()` switches level by reloading the game with another one, which the console's `scene` command uses. Each level is a plain scene of its own, so opening it in the editor shows just that level. Renders the world in a low-res `SubViewport` (about 480 lines, `main.gd`'s `target_lines`), upscaled with nearest filtering and run through the dither shader. Each low-res pixel is a whole number of physical screen pixels (3 × 3 on a 1440p screen), so they all come out the same size; the container is scaled rather than using `stretch_shrink`, which only takes whole canvas units and would land on 360 lines there. |
| `scenes/world.tscn` | Sky, fog, sun and the player (`main.gd` adds the level). The mood is a clear midday: a blue sky fading to a pale horizon with fair-weather clouds drifting across it (`materials/sky.gdshader`), a warm golden sun about 30° up (low enough to sit in view and cast long shadows; four cascades out to 140 m), a softer cool blue fill light taken partly from the sky so shade reads blue rather than grey, and light blue-grey distance fog that starts at 45 m and glows toward the sun. ACES tonemapping (exposure 1.2, white 4.5) rolls off the highlights instead of clipping them, with a light contrast and saturation lift, so sun and shade stay distinct rather than washing out. Screen-space AO is deliberately off: at the low render resolution it crushes whole shadowed areas to black. A thin volumetric haze softens the distance, and the `GroundMist` fog volume adds a light mist that pools near the ground and thins with height. |
| `scenes/hub.tscn`, `scripts/hub.gd` | The hub, where the game starts (`scene hub` in the console): a 160 m square field of dead flat grass, painted like the other levels' grassy ground (`scripts/flat_ground.gd`, shared with the gallery), with invisible walls round its edge so you can't walk off it. Ahead of you as you start stand three portals with the names of the other levels above them, **Gallery**, **Main Game** (the valley) and **Island**; walking through one takes you there. The ground and walls are built in the script (also in the editor); the portals and `PlayerStart` are ordinary nodes in the scene, to move or add to in the editor (a portal's `level` says where it goes and its `title` what's written over it). |
| `scenes/level.tscn` | The valley, the main level (`scene world` in the console): a 184 × 184 m valley of rolling hills ringed by cliffs and mountains. Pines grow in groves with open meadows between them, so you can see across the land. A creek (`terrain_creek` tiles) crosses the south of the valley from cliff to cliff along z = 64, just south of the outpost and the homestead. It flows west to east, the main road crosses it on a wooden bridge, and it cuts a small gorge through the rising rim at each end. Coils of barbed wire line the front and west side of the outpost, narrow the west road into a roadblock and lie about the ruins. A checkpoint guards the west road by the roadblock: two watchtowers either side of it, joined by a plank bridge over the road. It's generated by `tools/build_level.gd` but is an ordinary scene you can edit. The builder scales scenery up from the pack's real-world sizes (`WORLD_SCALE`): trees about 2–2.4×, cliffs 2×, ruins 1.6×. Things the player interacts with stay near human scale. |
| `scenes/gallery.tscn`, `scripts/gallery.gd` | Model gallery level (`scene gallery` in the console): a dead flat grassy field (built in the script at height 0 and painted with the palette's grass swatch like the levels' ground, so every model stands level) showing every model in `assets/`, one row per category (weapons, pickups, ammo, viewmodel, enemies, scenery, terrain, front to back). Each row has its category name above it and each model its file name. Small categories are enlarged (the heading says by how much) and weapons are turned side-on. The campfire burns with the game's fire effect (`scripts/campfire.gd`), and the golem floats on its electric arcs. Models, names and headings are set onto the grass's gentle bumps. The air is clear: the world's fog, haze and ground mist are off there. A wooden fence runs round the edge of the field to show where it ends, backed by invisible walls so you can't jump it and fall off. It reads the asset folders when it loads, so new models appear on their own. Opening `scenes/gallery.tscn` in the editor shows the field with every model laid out. |
| `scenes/island.tscn`, `scripts/island.gd` | Island level (`scene island` in the console): a small island in an open sea. The island is about 85 m across with a ragged coastline: a beach 10 m wide rising gently from the water to grassy hills up to about 4 m high, with rocks along the shore, a few trees and bushes inland, and on the hilltop the ruins of an overgrown gateway with glowing rune channels (`assets/scenery/ruins_gate.glb`) among broken walls, columns and rubble, on ground levelled round them. Everything standing on the island is an ordinary node saved in the scene, to move, turn, add or delete in the editor: the rocks, trees, bushes and grass under `Props`, the ruins under `Ruins` (move the group to move the whole site), the creatures, and `PlayerStart`, where you start and come back after dying. The creatures show their models in the editor as previews (not saved). The ground is levelled round wherever `Ruins` stands; after moving it, press **Rebuild ground** in the Island node's Inspector (or reopen the scene). The shadow cat (`scripts/enemies/shadow_cat.gd`) roams the island, starting on the beach near you, and clubs you with its spiked tail if you get inside its aggro ring. The lynx ranger (`scripts/enemies/lynx_ranger.gd`) potters about on dry land a little way up the beach from where you start, and watches you when you come near. A dachshund (`scripts/enemies/dachshund.gd`) trots about too, runs over to greet you and sits pretty at your feet. The arcane eye (`scripts/enemies/arcane_eye.gd`) floats about over the island, watching you, and a big jellyfish (`scripts/enemies/jellyfish.gd`) swims through the air over the island and out over the shallows. A pair of lovebirds (`scripts/enemies/lovebird.gd`) fly about over it and perch on the trees, rocks and ruins. Under the water the sand shelves gently through the shallows, then drops steeply to 9 m, so the sea turns from turquoise to deep blue a little way out. The ground, sea and wading walls are built from `height()` when the level loads (also in the editor); the ground is one mesh with trimesh collision. It's painted like the main level's ground: the palette material dimmed to the same `ground_brightness`, with each 1 m face stretched over one swatch the way the asset generator maps the tiles (so it shows the swatch's grit), sand on the beach and under the water and grass inland, so the grass edge steps along the faces. You can wade out until the water is about 1 m deep, where invisible walls follow the coast round. The player starts on the south beach looking out to sea, with surf washing in (`assets/sfx/surf_loop.wav`). |
| `scenes/player.tscn`, `scripts/player.gd` | FPS movement (CharacterBody3D): quick acceleration and braking, air control that keeps momentum, sprint, crouch, sprint-slide, a jump with coyote time, input buffering, variable height and a floaty apex, vaulting onto ledges up to 80 cm above the feet from the air, stepping up ledges up to 35 cm, and climbing ladders. It emits `jumped`, `landed`, `vaulted`, `slide_started` and `slide_ended`. |
| `scripts/ladder.gd` | `Ladder`: an Area3D that marks a climbable ladder and tells the player when they're on it. Its origin is the ladder's foot, +Z points out toward the climber, and the landing (a tower's deck) starts `landing_gap` behind it. `tools/build_level.gd` puts one on every watchtower. |
| `scripts/camera_effects.gd` | Camera feel, purely visual: head bob with sway and foot plants, leaning into strafes and slides, FOV kick when sprinting and sliding, a dip and FOV pop on jumping, tipping with vertical speed in the air, a pull-up on vaults, springy landing dip, shake on hard landings and idle breathing. |
| `scripts/jump_dust.gd` | Dust kicked up at the feet on jumping, vaulting and landing (bigger for harder landings). |
| `scripts/player_audio.gd` | Footsteps synced to the bob, plus jump, landing and slide sounds. |
| `scripts/weapons/gun_recoil.gd` | `GunRecoil`: a held gun's recoil as damped springs on its position and rotation. A recoil of 1 (the pistol) kicks about 7 cm back and 13° up, with a random twist and a roll that throws each hand's gun outward. Bigger guns kick harder, but less than in proportion (recoil to the power 0.7), so the rocket launcher stays on screen. In rapid fire each kick only has the room left under a cap of 1.3 kicks, so automatic weapons shudder rather than climb. |
| `scripts/weapons/weapon_manager.gd` | Weapons inventory, ammo (bullets, shells, rockets, cells, each with a carry limit), firing and the held weapon. The held weapon is drawn by its own camera into an overlay so it never clips into walls, and it sways, bobs, recoils and switches with animation. Each shot snaps the gun back and up with a random twist and roll that springs home with a little overshoot (`gun_recoil.gd`, scaled by the weapon's `recoil`), the view kicks and shakes slightly, and bullet and shell weapons flip spent casings out of the side (brass, or red shotgun hulls). First-person arms (`assets/viewmodel/`) hold each weapon. Every `WeaponData` sets where the right hand grips and whether the left hand cradles a barrel or handguard (`support`) or holds a front grip (`grip`). The forearms run from each wrist to a shoulder point just below the view, so the arms follow sway, recoil and switching. On a ladder the weapon is lowered out of sight and a second pair of grip hands climbs the rungs (`_update_climb()`): each is placed where its rung appears on screen, so the hands hold the real ladder. A weapon with `dual_wield` set (the pistol) can be carried twice: picking up a second one puts it in the left hand, mirroring the right, each gun held in one hand. Shots alternate between them, each from its own muzzle with its own flash and kick, about 1.7 times as fast as with one (`dual_interval_scale`), drawing on the same ammo. |
| `weapons/*.tres` | One `WeaponData` per weapon (pistol, shotgun, rifle, rocket launcher, plasma rifle): model and hold pose, fire rate, pellets, spread, recoil, ammo and projectile look. Each projectile's `projectile_size` (radius) drives its trail, muzzle flash and impact explosion, so they all scale together: shotgun pellet < pistol < rifle < plasma < rocket. |
| `scripts/weapons/projectile.gd`, `explosion.gd`, `fx.gd` | Glowing projectiles with particle trails (and smoke for rockets) that raycast each step. Rockets have a proximity fuse (`proximity_radius`) that sets them off when they pass within 1.5 m of an enemy, so they still catch fast, strafing eyes, plus explosions (fireball, smoke, sparks, light flash, sound, camera shake) scaled by the weapon. |
| `scripts/physics_prop.gd` | Makes the barrels physics objects. On load, each swaps its imported static collision for a `RigidBody3D` with a cylinder fitted to the model, which carries the mesh. It stands frozen until first hit, so barrels on slopes don't roll away by themselves and idle ones cost nothing. Projectiles push any rigid body they hit at the point of impact (`KNOCK_PER_DAMAGE` in `projectile.gd`). `blast()` throws props up and away from rocket splash and gas-can explosions, capped at `MAX_THROW_SPEED` so several blasts at once don't send one out of sight. `tools/build_level.gd` puts it on every `barrel`. |
| `scripts/explosive_barrel.gd` | `PhysicsProp` for `barrel_explosive`: knocked about like the plain barrels, and blows up like a gas can (`gas_can_shots` hits, or any blast nearby), with a 1.4× fireball, fire and smoke. The blast itself is `GasCan.fuel_blast()` and `GasCan.blast_at()`. Cans and barrels share the `explosives` group, so either sets the other off. |
| `scripts/weapons/pickup.gd` | Weapon, ammo, health and powerup pickups that respawn (weapons after 30 s, ammo after 15 s, health after 25 s, the invulnerability powerup after 60 s), and gems, which glow green and are taken for good with a chime and a sparkle. They're placed by `tools/build_level.gd`. With `random_spot` set (every weapon but the pistol, and the powerup), a pickup moves to a random open spot when the game starts and on each respawn: it casts down from above at random points inside the valley and takes the first one where it hits the bare ground (`Terrain.tile_at()` rules out pond and creek tiles), the slope is gentle, a 1 m sphere above it touches nothing, and it is at least 24 m from the other random-spot pickups and 15 m from the player. Enemy loot uses the same pickup as a one-time drop that blinks out after 30 s. Weapons glow in their projectile color so you can spot them from across the valley: a beam of light rises from each and a soft halo surrounds it (`materials/pickup_beam.gdshader`, one shader for both; they widen with distance to keep their size on screen, ignore fog and fade out past about 110 m), the model shimmers and a light pulses under it. The beam and halo are on visual layer 4, which the minimap leaves out. |
| `scripts/shield_veil.gd`, `materials/shield_veil.gdshader` | The invulnerability shield (`ShieldVeil` under the Player). Player-side, `give_powerup()` starts or tops up `invulnerable_time` and `take_damage()` turns everything away while it runs, emitting `shield_hit`. The veil is a small sphere around the camera, seen from inside, that keeps its world orientation. Its shader draws thin interference ripples, posterized into flat steps, that thicken toward the screen edges. It's added on top of the scene with depth testing off, so walls never cut it. A faint cyan light, a hum loop and the `shield_up` / `shield_down` chimes go with it (synthesized by `../tools/generate_sfx.py`). |
| `scripts/enemies/enemy.gd` | `Enemy`, the base class of the enemies: aggro and deaggro (it notices you within 5 m, `aggro_radius`, if it can see you, or when you shoot it; it gives up once you are 20 m away, `deaggro_range`, or out of sight for a few seconds, `memory`, or once the chase takes it 30 m from home, `leash`, and drifts back home), the see-through dark red ring on the ground marking its aggro radius (a `Decal` that follows it over the terrain; enemies and rings are on visual layer 3 so the ring skips its enemy and the minimap leaves both out), the hit flash and knockback, death, loot (ammo for weapons you own, or health, plus gems: `gem_drops`, 1 per eye or spider and 3 per golem; drops blink and vanish if not taken within 30 s, `loot_lifetime`) and respawning at home out of the player's sight. |
| `scripts/enemies/floating_eye.gd` | The floating eye enemy. It hovers near home until you come within 5 m, then screeches and chases, holding about 11 m away and strafing. It glows and whines before spitting a slow, dodgeable bolt (`weapons/enemy_eye_bolt.tres`). Its rigged tentacles are animated in code: a wave runs down each one, they trail behind as it moves, flare out while it charges and twitch when hit. It has 80 health, flashes and recoils when hit, bursts on death, drops ammo for weapons you own or health plus a gem, and respawns at home after 45 s. Nine are placed around the map. |

| `scripts/enemies/toxin_spray.gd` | `ToxinSpray`, the spider's spit: a hose-like stream of milky white droplets (170 a second, in a tight cone that wavers like an unsteady hose), lobbed on the flatter arc that lands them on you under gravity and re-aimed every frame, with a fine mist spraying out alongside. | `scripts/enemies/shadow_cat.gd` | `ShadowCat`, the shadow cat (`assets/enemies/enemy_shadow_cat.glb`, 90 health), which lives on the island. The body bounds and rocks nose-up and nose-down, the head reaches forward and low (held steady against the rocking), the ears pin back and the tail streams out behind. The gait cycle advances with the distance covered, so planted paws don't slide. Each paw is set on the ground below it (a raycast) and the legs bend to reach it (two-bone IK). Paws curl as they swing, and the body crouches slightly and pitches with the slope. The tail gives a slow swish now and then when idle, the ears twitch and flop with its steps and lie back while it hunts, and for the attack the tail is aimed straight out bone by bone, trailing against the spin. |
| `scripts/enemies/lynx_ranger.gd` | `LynxRanger`, the lynx ranger (`assets/enemies/enemy_lynx_ranger.glb`), a chibi lynx kit in a navy ranger's coat who lives on the island. ## Updating assets

After regenerating the asset pack (see the root `README.md`), run:

```
python3 tools/sync_godot_assets.py                                              # copy models, write import settings
/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot --headless --path godot --import   # reimport
/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot --headless --path godot --script res://tools/build_prefabs.gd   # prefabs for new models
/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot --headless --path godot --script res://tools/build_level.gd   # optional: rebuild the level
```

Rebuilding the level overwrites `scenes/level.tscn`, including any hand edits.
