# Opus FPS - A experiment with Opus 5.5 creating an old school looking Godot based FPS

![In game: a rocket sets off the gas cans at the outpost, throwing barrels into the air, with a demon and a floating eye nearby](docs/screenshot.jpg)

A first-person shooter with a PS1 look, made in Godot 4. Hunt floating eyes, electric rock golems, toxin-spitting giant spiders and clawed treants across a hilly valley of pine groves, a village, an outpost and ruins. Or visit a small island in an open sea, where a shadow cat with a spiked tail prowls the beach, a pumpkin crab in a witch's hat scuttles along the sand, a lynx ranger in a navy coat potters about, an arcane eye and a big jellyfish drift overhead, and a chibi bat with huge orange eyes flits about the back of the island. Blow up gas cans and barrels, collect gems and step through linked portals. Every model and sound is generated by scripts in `tools/`.

The game is in `godot/`. See [godot/README.md](godot/README.md) for how to run it, the controls and how the code fits together.

## PS1-style asset pack

![Showcase scene built from the pack](assets/previews/showcase.png)

The models in `assets/` are generated by `tools/generate_assets.py`. Preview images in `assets/previews/` are rendered by `tools/render_previews.py`. To regenerate, run:

```
B=/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender
$B -b --factory-startup -P tools/generate_assets.py -- assets   # models + palette
$B -b --factory-startup -P tools/render_previews.py -- assets   # preview images
```

### Palette

Every model uses one shared 128×64 texture, `assets/palette.png`. It is an 8×4 grid of 16 px swatches with a little pixel noise. Each face's UVs fall inside a single swatch, so every face reads as one flat color. Glowing parts (flames, pickups, eyes, keys) use a second material, `PS1_Glow`, which samples the same texture as emissive color. A portal's membrane uses a third material, `PS1_Portal`, meant to be replaced in the engine by an animated see-through shader. A creek's water uses a fourth, `PS1_Water`, meant to be replaced by a flowing-water shader. In other engines it shows as flat see-through blue-green. A model can also have its own copy of the palette with a few swatches recolored, for colors the shared one lacks (`PALETTE_VARIANTS` in the generator, written as `assets/palette_<model>.png` and embedded in that model): the bat's is how it gets its soft indigo fur, the fallen tree's gives it warm bark and a pale cut face, the rain fountain's gives it polished brass and turquoise glass, and the belfry house's its warm plaster and terracotta. The jellyfish's bell uses a fifth, `PS1_Jelly`, smooth-shaded and meant to be replaced by a soft glowing shader; in other engines it shows as a flat glowing pink. Poured water, like the rain fountain's pool, spouts and spill, uses a sixth, `PS1_Flow`, whose UVs are flow coordinates in meters (u the way the water runs, v across it), meant to be replaced by a shader that scrolls ripples along them; in other engines it shows as flat glowing turquoise.

The palette is deliberately somber: cold deep greens, muddy earth, heavy stone and dried-blood roofs. The saturated accent row is kept for blood, fire, pickups and energy weapons so they stand out.

The texture sampler is set to nearest filtering. For the full PS1 look, also turn off mipmaps in your engine (e.g. three.js `texture.generateMipmaps = false; texture.minFilter = NearestFilter`). Vertex snapping and affine texture warping are shader effects and belong in the engine, not in these files.

### Conventions (glTF, Y-up, meters)

- **assets/terrain/**: 8×8 m tiles with their origin at the center. Each tile's edges sit at height 0, so tiles placed on an 8 m grid connect seamlessly. `terrain_cliff` is an 8 m wall segment that tiles along X. `terrain_mountain` is a 30 m backdrop piece. `terrain_creek` carries a creek along X, so creek tiles chain end to end. The channel is about 4 m wide at the water line and 0.75 m deep, and meanders gently between grassy banks. It holds boulders on the banks, stones breaking the surface and pebbles on the gravel bed. Its water is a flat sheet 0.4 m down using a fourth material, `PS1_Water`, whose UVs are in meters with u running downstream (+X), so an engine shader can scroll ripples along the flow.
- **assets/scenery/**: origin at ground level. `fence_wood` is a 2 m segment with a post only at its -X end, so use `fence_post` to cap the last segment. `stone_wall` tiles every 4 m. There are three bushes: `bush` is a low, spreading clump, `bush_short` is a knee-high leafy dome with red berries (about 1.1 m across, 0.7 m tall), and `bush_tall` is a head-high upright shrub, leafy almost to the ground (about 2 m across and 2 m tall). `grass_patch` is a clump of meadow grass for carpeting grassy ground (about 0.9 m across and 0.2–0.45 m tall): 56 thin blades bent over partway up, dark green at the base and lighter toward the tips with a few dry ones, thick in the middle and thinning at the edge so clumps blend together. Each blade has a front and a back face, so it shows from every side, and the whole clump is only 336 triangles so thousands can fill a meadow. `rock_mossy_pile` is a heap of mossy boulders (about 3.4 m across and 2.9 m tall to the tips of its leaves): big grey stones, rounded blocks chiselled into flat facets, stacked in three tiers under a crowning stone split in two, with cobbles packed round the foot. Olive-green moss, as on the ruins gate, cushions every upward face and creeps over the upper edges (each flat facet is one color, so it reads as a single chiselled face), and broad green leaves sprout from the top and from the crevices. Its leaves are two-sided, like the grass. `tree_fallen` is a fallen tree (about 4.1 m long and 3.1 m tall): a thick log lying along X, sawn off at its -X end to show pale growth rings inside a reddish bark rim, its +X end resting against the broken, ivy-clad snag it fell from, which forks into two broken branches at the top. A spiky tuft grows on the log by its sawn end, and on the +Y side a little stump wears a dome of leaves. It has its own palette for its warmer browns, and its leaves are two-sided, like the grass. `fountain_rain` is a rain fountain (about 3.6 m across and 2.5 m tall), its front toward -Y: a brass-bound glass globe with a raincloud inside, on a cream column ringed with brass, standing on a stone plinth in a round pool walled with mossy stone blocks. Three brass spouts pour into the pool and a stream spills over a low stone at the front of the wall. Little glass vials hang off the globe on brass pipes, with a porthole, a valve wheel, and pearls on the cap and vials. Boulders, leaves and white flowers ring its foot. Its glass and pearls glow, its water is `PS1_Flow` so it pours and spreads, and it has its own palette for polished brass, turquoise glass and blue-grey stone. `house_belfry` is a white plastered house with a steep terracotta roof and a little belfry on its ridge (about 8.4 × 7 m and 10.3 m tall to the belfry's spike), its front toward -Y: a gable end with a plank door. - **assets/weapons/**: origin at the grip, with the barrel pointing -Z (the direction the camera looks). They are real-world scale, so scale them up for first-person viewmodels.
- **assets/pickups/**: origin at the base, sized about 0.3–0.7 m. Spin or bob them in game.
- **assets/viewmodel/**: first-person arm parts: right and left hands closed around a pistol-style grip, a left support hand for barrels and handguards, and a forearm with a jacket sleeve. Each hand's origin is the centre of what it holds. The forearm's origin is the wrist, and it runs 0.7 m toward the elbow.
- **assets/ammo/**: one round per ammo type (bullet, shotgun shell, rocket, plasma cell) for the HUD ammo counter. Origin at the base, standing upright, real-world scale.
